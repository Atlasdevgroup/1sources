Every season leaves inventory behind. A brand overproduces a holiday gift set, a retailer cancels a spring order, a manufacturer switches to new packaging, or a discontinued product still has pallets in the warehouse. None of that product is flawed; it simply no longer fits the plan it was made for. Seasonal and closeout inventory is how that name-brand merchandise reaches buyers who can still sell it.

For the right buyer, these lots are some of the best value in wholesale. Off-price and discount retailers build much of their appeal on recognizable brands at unexpected prices, and closeouts make that possible. Online sellers use them to add margin on items they already list. Exporters often sell out-of-season or previous-packaging goods into markets where the timing or packaging difference does not matter.

The trade-off is choice and time. Closeouts usually come as offered, in the mix and quantity available, and they rarely wait long for a decision. Seasonal goods and short-dated lots also carry a deadline for the buyer: the product has to sell before the season ends or the code runs out. Buyers who do well here know their sell-through rates and storage capacity, and can decide quickly whether a lot fits.
