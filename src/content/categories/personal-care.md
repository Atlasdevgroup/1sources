Personal care is the part of the store that shoppers restock without thinking about it. Toothpaste, deodorant, body wash, razors, diapers and feminine care move every week in grocery, pharmacy and convenience channels, and the price of each item is well known to the shopper. That makes the category a margin discipline more than a discovery exercise. A retailer that pays a little too much on fast movers feels it all year.

We treat personal care separately from health and beauty because the buying pattern is different. Beauty buyers chase shades and trends. Personal care buyers want the same core items, in reliable quantities, at a landed cost that holds up against the big-box price down the street. The interesting purchases are often packaging transitions, when a brand changes its carton and the previous version becomes available, and the bulky baby and incontinence lines, where freight planning matters as much as the unit price.

Most personal care buyers also stock health and beauty and household basics, and it rarely makes sense to buy a pallet of one brand's toothpaste on its own. 1Sources works on a single account, so a personal care order can sit on the same purchase order as shampoo, laundry detergent or snacks. Your account contact sends offers that match what you carry and quotes by the case, with the case pack, packaging version and any dating stated before you buy.

For online sellers, personal care rewards accuracy. Listings are often tied to a specific size, count and packaging version, and a mismatch creates returns and account problems. Tell us which versions your listings require. When an offer does not state the version clearly, ask, and confirm it before the order ships.
