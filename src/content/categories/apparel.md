Apparel is sold by size, not just by unit. A case of name-brand T-shirts is only as useful as its size breakdown, and two lots of the same style can be worth very different amounts depending on how many mediums and larges they contain. For that reason, wholesale apparel buyers look at a size breakdown before they look at a price.

Most apparel moves through two channels. The first is planned: basics, socks, underwear and sleepwear that stores keep in stock all year and reorder in consistent prepacks. The second is off-price: overstock, cancelled orders and prior-season goods that brands and retailers need to clear. Off-price lots are where most of the value is, but they come with fewer choices. Colors, sizes and styles are what they are, and the buyer decides whether the mix fits the store.

Timing is the other variable. Clothing follows the weather and the retail calendar, so heavy outerwear, swimwear and holiday items have narrow selling windows. An off-price retailer with storage space can buy out-of-season goods cheaply and hold them. A boutique with limited stockroom space generally wants product it can put on the floor within weeks.
