  - title: Age grading and small-parts warnings
    body: >-
      Toys are age-graded, and toys intended for children must meet the
      mandatory federal toy standard, ASTM F963. Products with small parts, small balls, marbles or
      balloons carry choking hazard warnings when intended for certain ages.
      Keep packaging intact, since the warning and age grade are part of what
      makes the product compliant to sell.

    body: >-

      demand drops and values change quickly. Plan quantities against the
      weeks you actually have left to sell.
  - title: Licensed product timing

The toy business runs on a calendar. A board game that sells slowly in April can be sold out by mid-December, and a licensed figure that everyone wants during a movie release can be hard to move three months later. Wholesale toy buying is mostly about timing: committing to the right quantities early enough to catch the holiday season, and knowing which lines will still sell after it.

Name-brand toys also come with a compliance layer that buyers should understand. Children's products sold in the U.S. need tracking labels, age grading and, where relevant, small-parts warnings, backed by testing and a certificate from the manufacturer or importer. Brand-name toys in original packaging carry all of that. That is one reason independent toy stores and online sellers prefer established brands to unbranded alternatives that are cheaper per unit but harder to stand behind.

Buyers in the category approach it in different ways. Toy and hobby stores buy wide assortments and care about depth in their best-selling lines. Discount and variety retailers want strong names at prices that support impulse purchases. Marketplace sellers usually focus on a narrow set of items with established demand and are careful about box condition, since a damaged package can mean a return. Pop-up and seasonal retailers buy almost entirely in the weeks before the holidays.
