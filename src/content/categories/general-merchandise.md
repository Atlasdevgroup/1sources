General merchandise is the catch-all of retail: the kitchen gadgets, storage bins, pet bowls, pens, extension cords, party plates and picture frames that fill the aisles between the food and the pharmacy. No single item carries a store, but together these products can drive a large share of a discount or variety retailer's sales and margin. The job is less about finding one winner and more about keeping a wide assortment fresh and correctly priced.

That shapes how the category is bought. A GM buyer might place one order covering dozens of items from different brands, each in small case packs, rather than a deep order of one product. Orders frequently ship as mixed pallets, which saves on freight and handling but calls for careful building and receiving. Items that are bulky or fragile, such as glassware and storage containers, need to earn their freight cost before they belong in the mix.

Price points matter more here than almost anywhere else. Many discount and dollar formats sell GM at fixed prices, so an item only works if its landed cost leaves a margin at that price. Buyers in these channels are quick to pass on a recognizable brand when the numbers do not work, and quick to add an unfamiliar item when they do.
