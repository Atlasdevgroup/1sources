A shoe lot is a grid, not a pile. Each style comes in colorways, each colorway in sizes, and sometimes in widths as well. Two offers with the same pair count can be completely different purchases once you see how those pairs are spread. Footwear buyers read the size curve before anything else, because a lot full of size 6 and size 14 will sell very differently from one centered on 9 and 10.

The box matters almost as much as the shoe. A name-brand sneaker in a crushed or mismatched box sells for less in a store and can be returned by an online customer who expected a gift-ready package. Retailers and marketplace sellers therefore pay close attention to how footwear is packed, stored and shipped. Master cartons protect boxes in transit; loose boxes on a pallet do not.

Most wholesale footwear buying falls between two extremes. Shoe stores and sporting goods retailers replenish core styles in reliable size runs. Off-price retailers and online sellers buy opportunistically: prior-season colorways, overstock, and lots where a brand has moved on to a new model. The second group accepts less choice in exchange for value, but still needs a curve it can sell.
