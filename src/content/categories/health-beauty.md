considerations:
  - title: Expiration and lot dating
    body: >-
      Sunscreens, OTC drugs and many skin treatments carry expiration dates, and
      cosmetics are coded by lot. Retailers generally want enough remaining shelf
      life to sell through, and marketplaces set their own dating rules for
      inbound stock. When a lot is short-dated, that should be stated on the
      offer so you can price and plan for it, rather than discovered when the
      pallet is opened.
  - title: Drug Facts labeling on OTC items
    body: >-
      Over-the-counter drugs sold in the U.S. must carry the FDA Drug Facts
      panel, and some products that look like cosmetics, such as sunscreen,
      anti-dandruff shampoo and acne treatments, are regulated as OTC drugs.
      Buy them in the original, unaltered retail packaging. Relabeled, repacked
      or foreign-language units create compliance problems for both stores and
      online sellers.
  - title: Shade and SKU fragmentation
    body: >-
      A single cosmetic or hair color line can run to dozens of shades, and
      offers rarely cover every one in equal depth. Check the shade breakdown
      against what your shoppers actually buy. A deep position in slow shades
      ties up cash, and planogram gaps in core shades are what customers notice
      first.
  - title: Hazmat on aerosols and nail polish
    body: >-
      Aerosol hair spray, dry shampoo, nail polish, remover and many fragrances
      are flammable and ship as limited-quantity hazardous materials. That
      affects packaging, carrier choice and, for online sellers, how
      fulfillment centers accept the product. Plan freight and inbound shipments
      with these items identified before the order leaves the dock.

Health and beauty is the most detail-heavy aisle in wholesale. A single case can hold fifty identical lipsticks in one shade, and a single offer can mix products that fall under different rules: a moisturizer is a cosmetic, while the same brand's SPF moisturizer is an over-the-counter drug with its own labeling requirements. Buyers in this category spend less time asking whether a product will sell and more time asking whether this particular lot, in these shades and with this dating, fits their shelf.

Most HBA buying is replenishment. A pharmacy restocks pain relievers and first aid on a steady cycle, a beauty supply store keeps its hair care and nail walls full, and a grocery HBA set turns over a few dozen fast movers. Opportunistic buys come on top of that: a discontinued shade line, a packaging change that leaves old-look inventory behind, or a seasonal sun care surplus after summer. Ecommerce sellers tend to buy narrower and deeper, focused on the items their listings already rank for.

1Sources handles the category through an account contact who sends offers and quotes, not through a public catalog. That fits HBA well, because the facts that decide a purchase are rarely on a product listing. Every offer should tell you what you need to judge the lot: case pack, condition and any short dating. If a shade breakdown or lot date matters to your decision, ask before you commit. It is a normal question, and the answer belongs on the quote.

Because health and beauty sits next to personal care and household goods on most shelves, many accounts combine the three on one purchase order. One account, one contact and one PO across several brands saves the back-and-forth of buying each line from a different vendor. If you already know which brands and segments you need, include them in your application so your first offers are relevant from the start.
